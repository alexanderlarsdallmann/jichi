# The emergency fund

*This is an educational description of a common budgeting concept, not financial
advice. Your situation is yours; for debt, investing, or a financial crisis, a
qualified professional is the right help.*

An emergency fund is money set aside for the unexpected — a car repair, a medical
bill, a gap between jobs — so that a surprise does not become a debt spiral. It is
the foundation most budgeting frameworks build **first**, before extra debt
payments or discretionary saving.

**Why first?** Because without a buffer, the next unexpected expense goes on a
credit card at high interest, which costs more than most saving earns. A small
cushion breaks that cycle and — just as importantly — removes a constant low-grade
stress. Peace of mind is part of the return.

**How to start without being overwhelmed:**

1. **A small first target, not the scary big one.** The classic "three to six
   months of expenses" is a *destination*, not a starting line. Aim first for a
   reachable milestone — enough to cover a typical surprise. Hitting a small goal
   builds the habit; staring at a huge one kills it.
2. **Automate a little, monthly.** A modest fixed amount from each month's budget
   (the "20" in 50/30/20, or its own envelope) beats waiting for a spare lump sum
   that never comes. Consistency matters more than size.
3. **Keep it separate and boring.** Somewhere you will not spend it by accident,
   easy to reach in a real emergency. This is not money to invest or chase returns
   with — its job is to *be there*, safe and calm.
4. **Define what counts as an emergency** before you need it, so the fund is not
   quietly drained for wants. A true surprise and necessity — not a sale.

Starting one at all is the win. A first funded buffer, however small, changes how
every other financial decision feels.
