# Envelope budgeting

*This is an educational description of a budgeting method, not financial advice.
Apply it to your own numbers; big money decisions belong to you and, when needed,
a qualified professional.*

Envelope budgeting is the oldest trick that still works: **decide how much each
category gets before the month starts, and when a category's "envelope" is empty,
you stop spending in it.** The name comes from literally putting cash in labelled
envelopes; you do the same with numbers in a file.

**How it works:**

1. List your spending categories — food, transport, going out, subscriptions,
   etc. Keep it to a handful; too many envelopes is unmanageable.
2. Give each an amount for the month, from your income after fixed costs and
   savings. The amounts must sum to what you have — that is the point.
3. As you spend, subtract from the envelope. When it hits zero, that category is
   done until next month (or you *consciously* move money from another envelope —
   which makes the trade-off visible, which is the whole benefit).

**Why it stops overspending:** it turns a vague "am I spending too much?" into a
concrete "this envelope is empty." The limit is decided when you are calm, not in
the moment of temptation. And moving money between envelopes forces you to *see*
the trade-off ("more eating out means less for the trip") instead of drifting.

**Making it stick:** start with a few broad envelopes, not twenty precise ones.
Round the numbers. Refill at the start of each month. The method only helps if you
actually update the envelopes as you spend — pair it with the spending tracker.
