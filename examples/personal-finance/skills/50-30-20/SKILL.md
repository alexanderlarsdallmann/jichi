# The 50/30/20 rule

*This is an educational description of a common budgeting frame, not financial
advice. It is a starting point to adapt to your own situation — not a rule you
must obey, and not a substitute for a professional when one is needed.*

50/30/20 is a simple way to split your **take-home pay** (after tax) into three
buckets, so a budget is quick to start without agonizing over every category:

- **50% — Needs.** The things you truly must pay: housing, utilities, groceries,
  transport to work, minimum debt payments, insurance. If it stops your life
  working, it is a need.
- **30% — Wants.** The things that make life nicer but are optional: eating out,
  streaming, hobbies, upgrades. Not bad — just the flexible part.
- **20% — Savings & debt.** Building an emergency fund, saving toward goals, and
  paying down debt beyond the minimums.

**How to use it:** total your take-home, split it 50/30/20, and see how your real
spending compares to those targets. The gaps are the insight — if "needs" are 70%
of your income, that is important to know, and the frame surfaces it fast.

**Where it bends (be honest):** the percentages are a *starting* guide, not a law.
On a low income, needs can genuinely exceed 50% and the split has to flex — that
is a real constraint, not a failure. With high fixed costs, or aggressive
debt payoff, the numbers shift. Use 50/30/20 to get *a* budget quickly, then
adjust it to your reality. A frame you adapt beats a rule you abandon.
