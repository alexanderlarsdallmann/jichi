description: Compare a month's actual spending to the budget — where you're on plan, where you drifted, and what to adjust. Not a judgement.
---
Review a period's spending against the budget, kindly and concretely. The goal is
insight the user can act on, not a verdict on their choices. Educational, not
financial advice.

Read `spending.csv` and `budget.md`, then:

1. **Total actual spending by category** and put it next to the planned amount.
   Show the difference for each — over, under, on plan.
2. **Point out the meaningful gaps**, largest first. State them as facts, not
   faults: "food was 40% over plan", not "you overspent on food". Where the money
   went is the user's business; whether the plan matched reality is the analysis.
3. **Two honest reads of a gap:** either the spending should change, or the
   *budget* was unrealistic and should change. Offer both; let the user decide.
   A budget that never matches reality is a bad budget, not a bad person.
4. **One or two small adjustments** to try next month — a category to re-plan, an
   envelope to cap, a subscription to reconsider. Small and sustainable.

Write a short `review.md` for the period. If a gap points at a real financial
problem (mounting debt, no income for essentials), say plainly that a qualified
advisor is the right next step — that is beyond a budgeting method.
