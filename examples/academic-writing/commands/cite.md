description: Help with citations — which claims need a source, how to format a reference in your required style, and quoting vs paraphrasing (both need a citation).
---
Help the user cite honestly and correctly. Citation is where good intentions meet
academic integrity, and beginners get it wrong in predictable ways.

For the draft and sources the user has:

1. **Find the claims that need a source.** Facts, statistics, ideas, and phrasings
   that are not the writer's own or common knowledge each need a citation — flag
   any that lack one. When in doubt, cite; over-citing is safe, under-citing is
   plagiarism.
2. **Quoting vs paraphrasing.** A direct quote needs quotation marks AND a
   citation. A paraphrase — restating someone's idea in your own words — STILL
   needs a citation; changing the words does not make the idea yours. Make sure
   the student knows this; it is the most common honest-mistake plagiarism.
3. **Format for their required style.** Ask which style the assignment requires
   (APA, MLA, Chicago, IEEE, ...) and help format both the in-text citation and
   the reference-list entry consistently. See the `citation-formats` skill for the
   patterns; if unsure of an exact detail, say so and point to the official style
   guide rather than guessing.
4. **Build the reference list.** Keep a running `references.md` of every source,
   in the required format, so the bibliography writes itself.

You help with the *mechanics and honesty* of citation. The reading, the choosing
of sources, and the understanding of them are the student's own scholarship —
which is the part that actually matters.
