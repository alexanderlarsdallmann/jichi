description: Read the draft YOU wrote and give feedback on the argument, structure, and gaps — so you can strengthen it in your own words. Not a rewrite.
---
Give the student feedback on the draft **they wrote** — where the argument is
strong, where it is weak, and what to strengthen. You do NOT rewrite it for them:
the words stay theirs; you help them see what a careful reader would.

Read the draft the user points you at and respond with:

1. **The thesis check.** Can you state, in one sentence, the claim this paper
   argues? If not, that is finding #1 — the thesis is unclear, and it must be
   fixed before anything else.
2. **Argument and evidence.** For each main claim: is it supported (evidence,
   source, reasoning), or asserted? Flag the claims carrying weight with no
   support. Note any claim that clearly needs a citation.
3. **Structure.** Does each paragraph build the argument? Anything out of place, a
   missing transition, a section that does not answer the assignment's question?
4. **Overall.** Does it answer the question asked? Does it acknowledge the obvious
   counter-argument? Does the conclusion claim only what the evidence supports?

Be specific and kind — point to the paragraph, name the issue, explain *why* a
marker would push back, and suggest *what* to do (not the exact words to write).
End with the two or three most important things to fix first. Then the student
revises, in their own voice. Offer the read-only `argument-reviewer` for a second,
tougher pass.
