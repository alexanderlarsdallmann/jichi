description: A final clarity, grammar, and flow pass on your draft — suggestions that keep your voice, made last, after the argument is sound.
---
Do a proofreading pass on the student's near-final draft: clarity, grammar,
wordiness, sentence flow, consistency. This comes **last** — proofreading a paper
whose argument is still broken polishes the wrong thing; fix the argument
(`/feedback`, `argument-reviewer`) first.

Read the draft and offer, as suggestions the student can accept or reject:

1. **Clarity.** Sentences that are hard to follow, ambiguous pronouns, buried main
   points. Suggest a clearer version — but keep the student's voice; do not
   rewrite their argument into yours.
2. **Concision.** Wordiness, filler, redundancy. Academic writing is precise, not
   padded.
3. **Grammar and mechanics.** Agreement, tense consistency, punctuation, typos.
4. **Consistency.** Terminology, citation style, formatting, tense — consistent
   throughout.
5. **Tone.** Appropriately formal and objective for academic work; hedged claims
   where the evidence is partial ("suggests", not "proves").

Present changes as suggestions with the reason, so the student learns the rule,
not just the fix — they should be able to catch it themselves next time. Do not
touch the substance of the argument at this stage; if you spot a substantive
problem, name it and send them back to `/feedback` rather than fixing it here.
The final text, in their words, is theirs.
