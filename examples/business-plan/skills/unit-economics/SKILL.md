# Unit economics

*This describes a way to think about numbers, not financial advice. For real
financial modeling, taxes, or funding, consult a qualified professional.*

The most important — and most skipped — question in a small business is not "how
big is the market?" It is: **does a single sale make money?** If one transaction
loses money, selling *more* of them digs the hole faster. Growth only helps a
business whose unit economics work.

**The three numbers, per customer:**

1. **Price** — what one customer pays (per sale, or over their lifetime if they buy
   repeatedly).
2. **Cost to deliver one unit** — everything it takes to fulfil one sale: materials,
   payment fees, your time (valued honestly — your hours are not free), per-customer
   hosting/tools. Price minus this is your **contribution margin** per sale. If it is
   *negative*, stop — no volume fixes losing money on every unit.
3. **Customer acquisition cost (CAC)** — what it costs to win one customer:
   advertising, samples, your time selling. Averaged across everyone you reach, not
   just the ones who buy.

**The check that matters:**

> Over a customer's lifetime, does what they pay you exceed (cost to deliver + cost
> to acquire)?

- For a one-time sale: `price - cost_to_deliver - CAC > 0`.
- For repeat customers: `lifetime value (LTV) > cost_to_deliver_total + CAC`. A
  common rule of thumb is LTV should be a few times CAC — but the real test is that
  the number is comfortably positive with honest inputs.

**Why beginners get burned:** they price by feel, forget to count their own time,
never estimate CAC, and assume "we'll figure out costs at scale." Then every sale
quietly loses money, and more customers means more losses. Do this arithmetic
*early*, with pessimistic inputs — and remember every number here is an assumption
to test (especially the price: willingness to pay is not what you hope, it is what a
real customer actually does). If the numbers only work at a scale you have no
credible way to reach, that is the finding, not a rounding error.
