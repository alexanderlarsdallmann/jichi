# Validation: test before you build

*This describes a method, not business/financial/legal advice.*

A business idea is a stack of assumptions, and most of them are wrong in ways you
cannot see from inside your own head. The mistake that costs the most is **building
the whole thing first and finding out after.** Validation is the opposite: find the
riskiest assumption and test it for the least money and time possible, *before*
committing.

**The mindset: "get out of the building."** You cannot validate an idea by thinking
harder about it at your desk. The evidence lives with real potential customers.

**Test the riskiest assumption first.** Not the comfortable one — the scary one,
usually: *do people actually have this problem, and will they pay to solve it?* If
that is false, nothing else matters. Testing it early is where you save the most.

**Cheap tests, roughly from cheapest up:**

- **Customer conversations** (nearly free): talk to 10-20 real potential customers
  about their *problem* — not your solution. Ask what they do today, what it costs
  them, what they have tried. Listen for pain, not politeness.
- **A landing page**: describe the offer, measure real interest (a sign-up with a
  reason, a "notify me").
- **A pre-order / deposit / waiting list with skin in the game**: the strongest
  cheap signal — will they *commit* (money, or a real deposit), not just say "cool"?
- **The "concierge" / manual version**: deliver the service by hand for a few real
  customers before building any product or automation. You learn everything, and
  build nothing you did not need.

**Real signals vs vanity metrics.** A vanity metric feels good and proves nothing:
page views, likes, sign-ups with no intent, friends saying "sounds great." A real
signal is someone **paying or committing** — putting time, money, or a deposit down.
Only real signals validate.

**Decide the threshold before you test.** What result means *go*, what means *pivot*
(change the idea), what means *stop*? A test where every outcome is "encouraging" is
not a test — it is reassurance. And the point: **finding out an idea does not work,
for a week and $0, is a success.** It is exactly the expensive mistake this saves
you from.
