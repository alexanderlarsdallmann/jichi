description: Understand the market and the real customer — who exactly has the problem, how badly, and what they do today. Not market research advice.
---
Help the user get specific about their **customer and market** — the box most
plans wave at and most businesses die on. "A big market" is not a customer; a
specific person with a specific, painful problem is.

Push past the vagueness, into `market.md`:

1. **Who, specifically?** Narrow "small businesses" or "gamers" to a real, describable
   group. The more specific, the more testable — and early customers are almost
   always a narrow niche, not everyone.
2. **What problem, how badly?** What exactly hurts, and how much? A "nice to have"
   rarely gets paid for; a "hair on fire" problem does. Is this urgent for them, or
   for you?
3. **What do they do today?** The alternatives — a competitor, a manual workaround,
   or nothing. "Nothing" can mean the problem is not painful enough to act on, which
   is important to know.
4. **How many, and how do you reach them?** A rough sense of size and of the channel
   to actually find them — not a giant TAM number, but "could I find 20 of these
   people this week to talk to?"
5. **The test that beats research:** the surest way to understand the market is to
   *talk to real potential customers* — see the `validation` skill. Note who you
   could talk to and what you would ask.

Keep it honest — mark what you *know* versus what you *assume*. A clear, narrow
customer with a real, urgent problem is worth more than a huge fuzzy market. This is
educational; for formal market research or legal/tax questions, consult a
professional. Offer the `assumptions-reviewer`.
