description: Turn your riskiest assumptions into cheap validation experiments — the smallest tests that could prove you wrong, before you build or spend.
---
Help the user turn their plan's **riskiest assumptions into cheap experiments** —
the validation milestones that tell them whether the idea is real *before* they
commit real money or months of building.

From the lean canvas, work through:

1. **List the assumptions, ranked by risk.** For each box, "if this is wrong, how
   badly does it hurt?" The riskiest is usually "customers have this problem and
   will pay" — start there. Do not test easy assumptions first because they are
   comfortable; test the scary one, because that is where you save the most.
2. **For the riskiest, design the CHEAPEST test that could show you are wrong.**
   Examples: talk to 10-20 real potential customers about the problem (not your
   solution); a landing page that measures real interest; a pre-order or a
   "waiting list with a deposit"; a manual/"concierge" version you deliver by hand
   before building anything. The bar is a *real signal* (someone pays or commits),
   not a vanity metric (page views, polite "sounds cool").
3. **Define what result means go / pivot / stop.** Before running the test, decide
   what outcome would convince you the assumption holds, and what would send you back
   to the drawing board. A test with no possible "no" is not a test.
4. **Order them as milestones** — cheapest and riskiest first — so you learn the
   most for the least, and can stop early if the idea does not survive contact with
   reality.

Write `milestones.md` — each an assumption, a cheap test, and the go/pivot/stop
threshold. Frame it kindly: finding out an idea does not work for $0 and a week is a
*win*, not a failure — it is exactly what this saves you from doing expensively. See
the `validation` skill. Offer the `assumptions-reviewer`.
