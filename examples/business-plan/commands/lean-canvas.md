Help the user fill in a **lean canvas** in `canvas.md` — the entire business idea
on one page. This replaces the 40-page plan: a long plan is mostly untested guesses
dressed as facts; the canvas makes the guesses visible so you can test them.

Work through the nine boxes, keeping each short and honest (a phrase, not an essay),
and mark anything that is a *guess* as a guess:

1. **Problem** — the top 1-3 problems the customer has. (Existing alternatives: what
   they do about it today.)
2. **Customer segments** — who *specifically* has this problem. Not "everyone" — a
   real, narrow group. (Early adopters: the ones who feel it most.)
3. **Unique value proposition** — the one clear reason this is worth their attention,
   in a sentence.
4. **Solution** — the top few features that address the problems (kept minimal).
5. **Channels** — how you reach the customer.
6. **Revenue streams** — how it makes money, and roughly the price.
7. **Cost structure** — the main costs to deliver it.
8. **Key metrics** — the few numbers that tell you it is working (real ones, not
   vanity).
9. **Unfair advantage** — what is hard for others to copy (often empty at first —
   that is honest).

As you fill each box, ask "is this known, or a guess?" — most are guesses, and
that is fine; the point is to see them. End by asking: **which box, if wrong, kills
the idea?** That is the riskiest assumption — the one `/milestones` will test first.
Offer the read-only `assumptions-reviewer` to find the guesses stated as facts.
This is a planning tool, not business/financial/legal advice.
