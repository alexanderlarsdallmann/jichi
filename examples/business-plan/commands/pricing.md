Help the user think through **pricing and unit economics** — does a *single* sale
make money? This is the arithmetic most beginners skip, and skipping it is fatal:
if one transaction loses money, more customers make things worse, not better.

Work through it in `pricing.md` (do the arithmetic and show it):

1. **The price** — what you would charge, and *why* that number. Anchored to what?
   (Cost-plus, competitor pricing, the value to the customer.) Mark it as the guess
   it is until tested.
2. **The cost to deliver ONE unit** — materials, per-sale fees, your time valued
   honestly, hosting/tools per customer. Revenue per sale minus this is your
   contribution per sale. If it is negative, stop and rethink — you cannot sell your
   way out of losing money per unit.
3. **Customer acquisition cost (CAC)** — roughly what it costs to get *one* customer
   (ads, your time, samples). A sale must eventually earn back more than it cost to
   win — over the customer's lifetime, if repeat.
4. **The honest check:** does `price - cost_per_unit - (acquisition cost per sale)`
   come out positive, at least over the customer's lifetime? If the numbers only
   work at a scale you have no plan to reach, that is a red flag, named now.
5. **Test the price, don't assume it.** Willingness to pay is an assumption like any
   other — a pre-order, a "would you pay $X?" conversation, or a real sale tests it
   (`validation` skill).

Keep the arithmetic visible and the assumptions marked. This is educational, not
financial advice; for real financial modeling, taxes, or funding, consult a
qualified professional. See the `unit-economics` skill for the full picture.
