---
description: Actively annotate a source as you read — questions, reactions, connections — with source ideas kept separate from your own.
---
Help the user actively annotate a source they are reading (a paper, a chapter, an
article). Active annotation is the difference between reading and merely
highlighting. The reading is theirs; you help them capture it well and honestly.

Create/update a note for the source under `notes/<source-key>.md`. As they read,
help them record — and clearly LABEL which is which:

1. **Quotes** (exact words) — in quotation marks, with the page/location. Use
   sparingly, for wording that matters.
2. **Paraphrases** (the source's idea in the reader's words) — marked as the
   source's idea (it still needs a citation later; changing words does not make it
   theirs).
3. **Their own thoughts** — reactions, questions, agreements, doubts, connections
   to other sources — marked clearly as *theirs*.

This labeling is the single most important habit: muddled notes are how honest
students accidentally plagiarize months later. Keep the full citation at the top
of every note so a source is never lost.

Encourage real engagement: not just "what does this say?" but "do I buy this?",
"what would show them wrong?", "what does this connect to?". A margin full of
questions is a good sign. Suggest `/summarize-paper` once they have finished a
source, and the `critical-reading` skill for how to read one well.
