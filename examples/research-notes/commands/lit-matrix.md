---
description: Build a literature matrix — sources down, themes across — so patterns, agreements, disagreements, and gaps become visible.
---
Help the user build (or extend) a **literature matrix** in `matrix.md` — the tool
that turns a pile of summaries into an organized understanding. A stack of
summaries is not a literature review; a matrix is how you see the field.

Structure it as a table: **sources down the side, dimensions across the top.** Good
dimensions depend on the field, but usually include:

- the **research question / focus** of each source;
- the **method / approach** (so you can compare like with like);
- the **key finding / claim**;
- and a few **themes** specific to the topic (the recurring questions the field
  argues about).

Fill a cell from the reader's summaries and notes (their reading, organized — do
not invent content for a source they have not read). Then read the matrix WITH
them, looking for what a list cannot show:

- **Agreement** — where sources converge (a settled point).
- **Disagreement** — where they conflict (a live debate, and a place for the
  reader's judgment).
- **Shared assumptions** — what everyone takes for granted (often worth
  questioning).
- **The gap** — what nobody has addressed, resolved, or studied. This is the most
  valuable thing a matrix reveals, because it is usually where the reader's own
  contribution belongs.

End by naming the two or three patterns and the gap the matrix makes visible.
Suggest `/synthesize` to turn these patterns into a written review.
