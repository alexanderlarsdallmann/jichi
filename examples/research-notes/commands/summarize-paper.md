description: Write a structured, critical summary of one paper — question, method, findings, limitations, and YOUR assessment.
---
Help the user write a structured summary of one paper they have READ (this works
from their reading and notes — it is not a substitute for reading the paper). Write
it to `summaries/<source-key>.md`.

Structure it so it captures both what the paper says and what the reader makes of
it:

1. **Citation** — the full reference, at the top.
2. **Question** — what problem the paper addresses, in one or two sentences.
3. **Method** — how they investigated it, briefly. Note anything that bears on how
   much to trust the findings (sample, design, how things were measured).
4. **Findings** — what they actually found, stated plainly — not the abstract's
   spin, the real result.
5. **Limitations** — what the paper admits, and what it does not (every study has
   boundaries).
6. **Your assessment** — clearly marked as the reader's own: does it convince you?
   How does it fit with, support, or contradict other sources you have read? This
   is the part that becomes your contribution.

Keep the source's claims and the reader's assessment visibly separate (mark which
is which), and keep the citation attached. A summary that is only sections 1-5 is a
book report; the value is in section 6. Suggest running the
`critical-reading-reviewer` if the summary feels like it only restates.
