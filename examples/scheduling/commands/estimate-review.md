---
description: Compare your time estimates to what things actually took — so you learn how wrong you are and plan honestly next time.
---
Run an estimate-vs-actual review — the habit that turns a chronic under-estimator
into a realistic planner. You cannot fix the planning fallacy by trying harder;
you fix it with data.

Read the learner's records (their schedule/timeblocks noted estimated vs actual
times) and:

1. **Line up estimate vs actual** for each recorded task. Compute the ratio (actual
   / estimate) for each.
2. **Find their personal fudge factor.** Across tasks, how much longer do things
   actually take? Most people land around 1.5-2x, but theirs is theirs — the point
   is *their* number, from *their* data.
3. **Spot the patterns.** Which *kinds* of task do they underestimate most (creative
   work? anything involving other people? "quick" fixes)? Underestimation is rarely
   uniform.
4. **Apply it going forward.** The next plan multiplies raw estimates by their
   measured factor. This one change makes plans that hold.

Write a short running `estimates.md` — the log, the current factor, the patterns.
Keep it up over weeks; the factor sharpens with data. Frame it kindly: being wrong
about time is universal and not a character flaw — *measuring* it is the skill, and
it is the difference between plans that work and plans that don't.
