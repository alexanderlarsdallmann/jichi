---
description: Block out a day into focused work sessions — protected time for deep work, matched to your energy, with breaks.
---
Help the user time-block a day: assign the day's tasks to specific protected time
blocks, so the plan is concrete and focused work actually happens. "I'll work on it
today" gets nothing hard done; "9:00-10:30, essay draft, phone away" does.

For the day:

1. **Identify the deep-work tasks** — the things that need real, uninterrupted
   focus — and give each a real block (60-90 minutes is a good unit; longer and
   focus fades). Put these in the learner's **peak hours** (`skills/time-blocking`).
2. **Batch the shallow stuff.** Email, admin, small errands into one or two blocks
   rather than scattered through the day interrupting focus.
3. **Protect the blocks.** Note what "protected" means for them — notifications off,
   phone away, one task only. A block invaded by interruptions is not a block.
4. **Schedule breaks and transitions.** Real breaks between blocks (you cannot focus
   for eight hours straight), and a little transition time — blocks that abut
   perfectly always overrun into each other.
5. **Do not overfill the day.** Three or four solid deep-work blocks is a *full*
   day for most people. Leave buffer.

Write the day's blocks with start/end times and the single task for each. Keep it
realistic — a day plan you follow beats a heroic one you abandon by 11am. Record
which blocks you actually kept, to feed `/estimate-review`.
