# Estimating time honestly

Here is a truth that will save you a lot of missed deadlines: **you consistently
underestimate how long things take.** So does everyone. It is called the *planning
fallacy*, and it is not a personal failing you can will away — it is how human
brains estimate. The good news: it is fixable, but only with data, not optimism.

**Why we underestimate:**

- We imagine the *smooth* path — the version with no interruptions, no bugs, no
  "oh, I also need to..." — and plan for that fantasy.
- We forget the small stuff (setup, breaks, switching, the last 20% that takes 80%
  of the time).
- We are optimistic on purpose; a big honest number is discouraging, so we shave it.

**The only cure is estimate-then-measure:**

1. **Estimate before you start.** Write down how long you think a task will take.
   Guessing is fine — the point is to have a number to compare against.
2. **Record the actual.** When it is done, note how long it *really* took.
3. **Compute the ratio** (actual / estimate). Do this for a couple of weeks of
   tasks.
4. **Find your fudge factor.** The average ratio is *your* number — most people
   land around **1.5-2x**, but yours is yours. Now multiply future estimates by it.

**Refinements that help:**

- **Break big tasks down.** You estimate a 2-hour task far better than a "2-week
  project." Estimate the pieces and sum them.
- **Notice which tasks you miss most.** Underestimation is not uniform — creative
  work, anything depending on other people, and "quick fixes" are classic
  blowouts. Adjust those extra.
- **Estimate a range, not a point.** "3-5 hours" is more honest than "4 hours" and
  reminds you uncertainty is real.

The whole discipline is one sentence: **stop trusting your gut about time; measure
it.** A planner who knows their real numbers makes plans that hold; one who trusts
optimism misses deadlines forever.
