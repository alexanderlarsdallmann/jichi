# Does a `refute` stage find what a "be critical" prompt does not? — the run (M637)

**Date:** 2026-09-17 · **Model:** `jlu/qwen3-coder-next` (HRZ gateway, free) — *the finding is
about this model* · **Pre-registration:**
[`proposals/2026-09-refute-ab.md`](../proposals/2026-09-refute-ab.md) (hypothesis, threshold,
planted-claim rules and what will not be concluded, fixed on 2026-09-16 before any run) ·
**Harness:** `tests/bench/refute_ab/` · **Verdict:** **the frame did work the words did not** — refute 12 of 12, control 1 of 12.

---

*Publication note: the model's answers and reports quote absolute paths; the account name in them is replaced by the placeholder `u` in the committed copies (`snapshot_lint`'s rule). Nothing else in the reports was changed after the planting.*

## What was fixed before the run, and what was not

Fixed by the pre-registration and followed: the model; twelve first-seat reports, one per
`src/util/*.c` file, produced once by a read-only `map` stage; one false claim planted by hand
in each, listed in `planted.tsv` before the A/B runs, of the three kinds the proposal named
(a wrong line number ×4, an inverted condition ×4, a function said to exist that does not
×4); the control arm's exact words ("Review the following critically and list anything
wrong:"); one run per report per arm, 24 runs; a hit read by a person, not a grep; the
threshold, 6 of 12 and more than the control.

Not fixed in advance, and decided before the runs: which twelve files (the model chose them
when it built the harness; all mid-sized, 248–504 lines), and where in each report the plant
sits (appended as one more numbered finding in the report's own style, so it does not stand
out by shape). Not achievable: a grader blind to their own planting. The same person planted
and graded; the sealed A/B mapping and the per-arm form are what stood between that person
and the arm, and the proposal names this as the main way the result can mislead.

## Results

| Arm | Named the planted claim as false or unsupported | False attacks counted | Mean wall per run | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `refute` (fixed frame, read-only subagent) | **12 of 12** | **4 of 45 attacks (9%)** -- counted 2026-09-17, after the run; see the last section | 14 s | 12 |
| control (`synthesize`, "review the following critically") | **1 of 12** | not assessed | 7 s | 12 |

Pre-registered threshold: refute names at least 6 of 12 **and** more than the control.
Both held, with room. Per planted kind: the refute arm caught all four invented functions,
all four wrong line numbers and all four inverted conditions. The control's one hit was a
wrong line number (`jc_runsview.c`, "line 512" in a 455-line file), which it called
"not in the provided excerpt … spurious". The control **endorsed** the other eleven
plants, most of them emphatically: "✅ Critical — and correctly identified" for a function
that does not exist (`diff_ops_shrink`), "Correct and **important** — undefined behavior on
64-bit" for a format mismatch on a line past the end of the file, "The initial review
missed a **critical bug**" for an inverted `pipe()` test that is not inverted. In one case
(`jc_assign.c`) the control reasoned for forty lines from the report's *quoted* line —
which was the planted falsehood — and never opened the source; in another (`jc_plan.c`) it
called the plant "a critical bug … must be confirmed in the source code" and did not
confirm it.

**False attacks were not assessed, and the column reads 0 for that reason.** Counting
claims an answer attacks that are *true* of the source requires verifying every one of
roughly sixty first-seat findings against the code; I did not do that, and a number I did
not measure does not belong in the table as if I had. What I can say from reading: the
refute arm's `jc_patch.c` answer declared all eight of the report's claims FALSE, including
at least one (a `strlen(old_s)` with no NULL check) that is at worst a contract question,
and its `jc_toolloop.c` answer dismissed a race-condition finding correctly (jichi is
single-threaded) with a reason that would be wrong elsewhere. The frame that produces
twelve hits also produces confident refutations of everything; whether those are right is
the measurement this run did not make, and it is the next one to make — pre-registered
the same day as the cross-model run (M640, [`proposals/2026-09-refute-ab.md`](../proposals/2026-09-refute-ab.md), last section), and made in M641: [`2026-09-17-refute-cross-model.md`](2026-09-17-refute-cross-model.md).

**The blind was formal, not real.** A `refute` answer begins `--- refutation (second seat)
---` and is organised under `## Rebutting`, `## Undercutting`, `## Stands`; a control
answer is prose with ticks and crosses. The arm was legible from the first line of every
file. The sealed mapping was not opened until `score`, and the per-arm form was filled from
the answers alone; but a grader who wrote the frame cannot un-know it. The result is not
in doubt at 12 against 1 — the difference is not the kind a biased reading manufactures —
but a future run should strip the frame's headings from the grading pack before a person
sees it, and the harness now says so in its README.

## Reading

The pre-registration asked whether the **frame** does work the **words** do not, and it
does, on this model, on these twelve plants: the same request — here is a review, is it
right? — answered under a frame that forbids agreement and forces three headings finds the
falsehood twelve times, and answered as "review critically" finds it once. The mechanism is
visible in the answers. The control's failure mode is not carelessness; it is *agreement*:
it reads the report as the thing to be improved, grades each finding, and accepts the
quoted code as the code — the `jc_assign.c` answer is forty lines of careful reasoning
about a line that does not exist. The refute frame's first instruction, "do not agree with
it", turns the same model to the source: every refute answer opened the file, and eleven of
twelve begin their verdict with what the file actually says at that line.

This is the M602 R2 recommendation — "ask the second seat to adversarially refute the
first" — surviving its first contact, where the craft A/B's prose-level instruction did not
survive its own. The difference between the two experiments is the difference the
argumentation program was built on: the craft section was *advice in the prompt*; the
refute stage is a *frame the author cannot weaken*, with a read-only fence behind it. The
control here **was** advice in the prompt, and it lost 1 to 12.

Two honest limits on the reading. First, the plants were of three easy kinds — a
nonexistent function, a wrong line number, an inverted condition — each checkable by
opening the file and reading one line, which is what the frame instructs; a subtler
falsehood (a wrong claim about behaviour that the code does have) was not tested and might
not fall so cleanly. Second, the reports were written by the same model that refuted them,
about code that model has read for months in this project's own dogfooding; the
pre-registration named that inflation risk, and it applies to both arms equally, which is
some comfort and not a control.

What changes because of this: `refute` stays a stage an author adds, as pre-registered —
but `docs/AGENT_COLLABORATION.md` and the M634 entry can now say it was measured, and the
number that goes beside it is 12 of 12 against 1 of 12, n = 12, one model. The next
measurement is the false-attack rate, because a refuter that names every plant by attacking
everything has found nothing, and this run could not tell.

## What this does not say

- Nothing about a different second seat: two seats, one base model, shared blind spots
  (M602's caveat). A null here does not say another model's refuter would fail.
- Nothing about unplanted errors: this measured recall on twelve known falsehoods of three
  kinds, in reports the same model wrote, about code it has read for months.
- Not a default. `refute` stays a stage a spec author adds.

## The false-attack count, made (2026-09-17, M641)

The column above read "not assessed" for the refute arm when this page was written; the
cross-model pre-registration (M640) fixed how it would be counted, and this section is that
count on the twelve existing refute answers. **An attack** is any claim in the report other
than the plant that the answer calls false, wrong, unsupported, undercut or "not a defect".
**A false attack** is an attack on a claim that is true of the source, decided by reading
`src/util/` **at the commit the reports were written against** (`dd30de1e`; M638 has since
rewritten one of the twelve files below the lines its report cites). The reader is the
person who planted the claims, as the pre-registration says.

**Total: 45 attacks, 4 false (9%), against 12 hits.** (M642 tested the four: the three `fprintf` claims
reproduce on `/dev/full` and are fixed; the dead store is a dead store. The count stands.) By the pre-registered bar -- fewer
false attacks than hits -- the baseline refuter **found rather than attacked**. The four
false attacks are two claims: an unchecked `fprintf` return at three sites, dismissed with a
reason ("fclose will report it") that the same file shows to be wrong, and a dead store
dismissed as speculation about intent when its factual half is true. Both are the smallest
kind of defect in their files, which is where a refuter told to disagree finds it cheapest to
disagree.

**What the count also shows, which the rate does not.** (1) Nine of the ten `jc_path.c`
attacks were one sentence: "minor style issues, documentation mismatches, or theoretical
concerns". Every one was right on inspection; none was inspected. A refuter that dismisses
by category is right exactly as often as the category is, and the frame did not stop it.
(2) `jc_cli.c`: the refuter accepted the report's one non-planted claim, and that claim is
false -- a bounded `jc_snprintf` cannot overflow. The false-attack rate does not see a false
acceptance, and this run had at least one. (3) Two captured answers (`jc_rss.c`,
`jc_runsview.c`) are missing their opening sections; the counts are over what was captured.
(4) The report of `jc_patch.c`, whose all-eight-FALSE verdict this page had singled out as
the likeliest place for false attacks, has none: all seven rebuttals hold, including the one
the page doubted.

**What this does not say.** One reader, who planted the claims. Attacks were counted per
claim as the answer addressed them; a blanket dismissal counts once per claim it covers, so
`jc_path.c`'s ten are as heavy as `jc_patch.c`'s seven inspected ones. A claim the report
had already talked itself out of ("no defect here") still counts as an attack when the
refuter rebuts it, which inflates the denominator in the refuter's favour by perhaps five.
