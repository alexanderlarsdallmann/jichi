# Module 10 — Teach it, teach with it

*Stage 3 (離（り） Ri) · ~4–6 h · assignment:
[`16-teach-a-peer`](../assignments/16-teach-a-peer.md) (4 pt) · map:
[CURRICULUM.md](../CURRICULUM.md)*

Two kinds of teaching, one module. First you teach the **tool** — encode
what you know into assets the agent carries into every session. Then you
teach a **person** — author a complete assignment for a peer, which will
humble you faster than any bug: you must remember what not-knowing felt
like ([JOURNEY.md](../JOURNEY.md)).

## The work

**1. Teach the tool.** Your `.jichi/` should by now be unmistakably yours;
if it still looks like the scaffold, this is the hour to change that. Four
asset types, in increasing order of authorship
([MEMORY.md](../MEMORY.md), [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md),
[RULES.md](../RULES.md), [SKILLS.md](../SKILLS.md)):

- have the agent `remember` three real conventions from your work so far;
- replace or extend the starter **glossary** with terms from *your* domain;
- add one rule to your `AGENTS.md` that encodes a preference you have
  actually defended (Stage 2's reflection, made durable);
- write one **skill** — a procedure you keep re-explaining, written once,
  loaded on demand.

This half is practice, not points — but do it before the graded half,
because writing for the agent is the warm-up for writing for a person: both
punish vagueness, only one complains.

**2. Change seats.** Work
[`16-teach-a-peer`](../assignments/16-teach-a-peer.md): a complete
assignment — brief, rubric, hint ladder, strict check, reference solution.
The meta-grader holds your check to the two-sided bar every grader in this
repository is held to: it must reject your task as shipped and accept your
reference solution. You have been on the receiving end of seventeen of
these; now you know why each part exists — and you will discover, wording a
rung, that "tune the ladder per level *is* the teaching" (deck 05) was not
a slogan.

**3. Watch a learner meet it.** Inflict your task on a peer — or, alone, on
a fresh agent session (`spawn` a solve, or just open a new session and load
your brief). Where they stall is information about your *brief*, not about
them. Revise once. This loop — author, observe, revise — is the whole
instructor's craft in miniature; the forthcoming instructor guide is that
loop written large.

**4. Close your own loop.** The tool-teaching flow has a mechanized version
you should run once on your record so far: `learn analyze` over your own
telemetry, `/learn` to draft lessons, `learn apply` — and, hardest,
**corrections**: retract a note you once saved with confidence and have
since outgrown ([LEARNING.md](../LEARNING.md)). A teaching you cannot take
back is dogma; asking the tool to forget your stale lesson is Ri's
homework.

## The gate

`16-teach-a-peer` `passed`, one real revision made after watching someone
(or a fresh agent) attempt your task, and at least one `.jichi/` asset of
each kind above authored in your own bench.

## Reflection

*(from [JOURNEY.md](../JOURNEY.md))* — teaching the tool is rehearsal for
teaching people. The gate you wrote for your peer is also the last check
this curriculum ever grades you on writing; from here, every gate in your
life is one you chose.

> **If you are stuck alone:** if your check keeps failing the meta-grader's
> "rejects as shipped" step, your task ships solved — separate the starting
> state from the solved state (that is what solution.sh is for). If your
> ladder reads like a solution in three fonts, delete rung 3 and re-ask:
> what QUESTION is the learner stuck on at that moment?
