# Module 11 — The capstone

*Stage 3 (離（り） Ri) · one to two weeks of real work · assignment:
[`17-capstone`](../assignments/17-capstone.md) (3 pt floor + the rubric) ·
map: [CURRICULUM.md](../CURRICULUM.md)*

A bounded real project, in a repository of your own, driven the way the
whole course taught — and a **portfolio** that proves it. This is the only
module whose subject the curriculum does not provide: the itch must be
yours, because judgment about *what is worth building* is the one skill no
fixture can carry.

## The work

The assignment brief and its
[proposal template](../assignments/17-capstone/proposal-template.md) carry
the mechanics; the module adds only the framing:

**1. Choose small, choose real.** One tool you want, one itch in a codebase
you have. The rubric rewards a scope *held*, not a scope survived. Every
capstone that fails, fails at this step, in week one, quietly.

**2. Propose before building** (M6): goal, scope **and non-goals**, the
envelope you will run under, the verify strategy — written before code, and
honest about what would make your own gate hollow (M5/M14: you know
exactly how gates go hollow now; say how yours could, and how you will
notice).

**3. Build in the braid** you have practiced: interactive turns for shape,
bounded `--auto` runs for grind (M8), review and refactor as you go (M7),
the record catching what goes wrong (M4/M9 — something will; that is
material, not failure).

**4. Assemble the portfolio**: the proposal, one representative run journal
(ended ok, verified, in scope), and two new record entries. The floor
checks the structure mechanically; the *project* is judged by the rubric —
`/check` alone, an instructor in a course, and best of all **live**:
explain a decision, change something on request, show the check that would
catch its removal. Process provenance beats artifact inspection, which is
why the journal — not the code — is the portfolio's spine.

## The gate — the last one

`17-capstone`'s floor `passed`, and the rubric judged — by `/check` and
your own honest read if you are alone, by your instructor in a course.
With that, the graded road ends: **all four stages, every gate**.

## Reflection

*(from [JOURNEY.md](../JOURNEY.md), and it is the course's last word)* —
you have arrived when your students break your forms, and it pleases you.
The curriculum's forms are now yours to break: re-tune the ladders, re-cut
the modules for your team, author the set this course didn't. The record
you started in Module 4 is the only artifact that must outlive all of it.
Read it once a year. That is the rest.

> **If you are stuck alone:** stuck choosing → the template's Goal section,
> one paragraph, three candidate projects, pick the one whose *verify
> strategy* you can write today. Stuck mid-build → shrink to the last green
> checkpoint and take a smaller step; the envelope was telling you the plan
> was too big (your Stage-2 record probably already contains this lesson
> in your own words). Finished but unsure it is "enough" → run the live
> test on yourself: change one behaviour on a whim; if your gate catches
> what should be caught and your journal shows what you did, it was enough.
